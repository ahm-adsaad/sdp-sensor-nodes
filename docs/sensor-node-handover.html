What this is

A battery-free environmental sensor node for our senior design project. Solar panels feed an energy-harvesting chip that charges a supercapacitor, and the node runs entirely off that stored charge.

It measures temperature and humidity with an SHT41, and air quality with an ENS160, then sends the readings over LoRaWAN. The main board carries an ATmega4809 microcontroller, an SX1276 radio module and an AEM10330 harvesting chip. The two sensors sit on their own small breakout boards and connect to the main board with jumper wires.

We need a 3D printed case to hold all of it together so the node can be deployed. Everything below is the parts list and the measurements for that case.

What the case has to hold

  • The main circuit board, with its breakaway rails still attached
  • The supercapacitor pack, wired to the board
  • The SHT41 and ENS160 breakout boards, which need to reach outside air
  • The antenna, which connects to the board through a short coaxial lead
  • One to three solar panels, which must face the sky

The solar panel gives 3.0 V and 50 mA in full sun, and its terminals are strips of foil tape rather than wires. The sensor breakouts connect to the board with ordinary jumper wires.
